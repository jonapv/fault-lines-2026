## European Macro-Financial Stress Thesis — 2026/27

Original thesis • information cutoff: 25 October 2026

As of 25 October 2026, my hypothesis is that European financial markets are underpricing the simultaneous accumulation of macro-financial vulnerabilities across the region.

My base case is that market resilience persists through the November electoral cycle, but I believe the probability of a severe repricing rises materially in the subsequent period, particularly from December 2026 through the first half of 2027.

I do not assume a specific trigger. The relevant risk is that an energy, sovereign, credit, liquidity, geopolitical, operational, confidence or other exogenous shock activates feedback mechanisms among vulnerabilities that already exist.

The risk scenario is therefore not simply a liquidity crisis. It is a potential polycrisis: multiple stress channels that may remain individually manageable but whose interaction could produce nonlinear macro-financial effects.

This is an author hypothesis, not a measured probability, institutional forecast or investment recommendation. Official evidence supports the existence of vulnerabilities; it does not establish the post-election timing or market underpricing asserted here. Election dates are observation markers, not a demonstrated causal mechanism.

The primary observation window is 1 December 2026–30 June 2027. A qualifying severe-repricing event must first cross the predefined threshold within that window. A threshold first crossed before 1 December does not confirm the post-election timing claim, even if the market remains below that threshold afterwards.

Failure to observe a qualifying severe repricing by 30 June 2027 counts against the primary timing claim even if underlying vulnerabilities persist. A later event cannot retroactively confirm this forecast. Stronger bank buffers, falling energy prices, easing credit standards and improving confidence are relevant counterevidence.

A severe equity repricing alone does not confirm a polycrisis. The evaluation protocol in METHODOLOGY.md requires severe repricing and material deterioration in at least three distinct non-market channels. The protocol is a transparent descriptive convention, not a probability model or a universal economic definition.
