01 / THE THESIS HYPOTHESIS

The risk is not one fault line.
It is how they interact.

European markets may be pricing a calmer outlook than the underlying risks suggest.

My base case is that markets remain resilient through November’s electoral cycle. After that, I expect the risk of a sharp correction to rise materially, particularly between December 2026 and June 2027. I am not predicting a single trigger: the concern is that a shock in one area — energy, public finances, credit, geopolitics or confidence — reinforces stress elsewhere.

Official data show the vulnerabilities. They do not prove my timing, the probability of a crisis or a causal link to elections.

04 / TRANSMISSION INFERENCE

A shock starts the chain.
Feedback can amplify it.

  1. External shock
  2. Energy → inflation
  3. Tighter financing
  4. Sovereign stress
  5. Credit → liquidity
  6. Market repricing

↺ Weaker growth can feed back into sovereign and credit stress.

An energy shock can raise inflation and squeeze real incomes; tighter financing may then expose fiscal and credit vulnerabilities. Policy buffers can interrupt the chain.

ECB institutional context. In its May 2026 Financial Stability Review (Overview, p. 6), the ECB links geopolitical and fiscal risks, non-bank liquidity and leverage exposures, and bank credit, liquidity and funding vulnerabilities. THE COUNTERWEIGHT

Fragility does not make a crisis inevitable.

Bank capital and liquidity buffers have improved. Corporate credit tightening undershot expectations. Brent retreated during October.

These facts can coexist with vulnerabilities. A credible stress thesis must allow resilience to win.

07 / THE TEST SCENARIO

What would change the verdict?

The clock runs out on 30 June 2027. A later crisis cannot retroactively validate this window. Nor can a pre-December threshold crossing qualify through persistence into December.

01 / RESILIENCE

The thesis weakens.

No qualifying first ≥20% threshold crossing, with material improvement in at least three numeric channels. Falling inflation, easier credit and recovering confidence matter.

02 / STRESS WITHOUT CRASH

The system absorbs it.

At least three channels deteriorate, but no qualifying first ≥20% threshold crossing. Vulnerabilities were relevant; the severe-repricing timing claim failed.

03 / POLYCRISIS

Stress becomes systemic.

A first ≥20% threshold crossing during December–June, plus deterioration in at least three distinct non-market channels within the defined observation and co-occurrence windows.

Mixed / unresolved: severe repricing without broad deterioration, fewer qualifying channels, or missing evidence. Never force these outcomes into “confirmation”.

Open the pre-specified decision rules

Benchmark: EURO STOXX 50 (SX5E), EUR price return. Running peak begins with the cross-checked 23 October 2026 close of 6,068.22. D = 100 × (close / running peak − 1). The episode’s first ≥20% threshold crossing must occur during 1 December 2026–30 June 2027. An earlier crossing cannot qualify merely because prices remain below the threshold in December. An episode resets only when the running peak is regained; crossing back and forth below the peak does not create a new episode. Author conventions, not universal definitions. Qualifying non-market deterioration:

  • Sovereign / fiscal vulnerability: EA debt/GDP ≥89.8% in a subsequent quarterly release.
  • Energy/inflation: final headline HICP ≥4.3% for two consecutive months.
  • Credit/liquidity: actual enterprise BLS net tightening ≥12%.
  • Real economy/confidence: CCI ≤−19.9 for two consecutive releases.
  • Operational: a newly documented cross-border supply, payments, settlement or financial-infrastructure disruption, verified by public authorities.

At least three flags must be active at one month-end, with the qualifying first threshold crossing that month or in the preceding two months. Use only releases available by 30 June. Related indicators within one channel count once; channels are not statistically independent.

Resilience uses the opposite moves: debt ≤87.8%, HICP ≤3.3%, BLS ≤2%, CCI ≥−15.9, with matching persistence rules. See the full protocol for vintage, missing-data and sensitivity rules.

European Macro-Financial Stress Thesis — 2026/27

Original thesis • information cutoff: 25 October 2026

As of 25 October 2026, my hypothesis is that European financial markets are underpricing the simultaneous accumulation of macro-financial vulnerabilities across the region.

My base case is that market resilience persists through the November electoral cycle, but I believe the probability of a severe repricing rises materially in the subsequent period, particularly from December 2026 through the first half of 2027.

I do not assume a specific trigger. The relevant risk is that an energy, sovereign, credit, liquidity, geopolitical, operational, confidence or other exogenous shock activates feedback mechanisms among vulnerabilities that already exist.

The risk scenario is therefore not simply a liquidity crisis. It is a potential polycrisis: multiple stress channels that may remain individually manageable but whose interaction could produce nonlinear macro-financial effects.

This is an author hypothesis, not a measured probability, institutional forecast or investment recommendation. Official evidence supports the existence of vulnerabilities; it does not establish the post-election timing or market underpricing asserted here. Election dates are observation markers, not a demonstrated causal mechanism.

The primary observation window is 1 December 2026–30 June 2027. A qualifying severe-repricing event must first cross the predefined threshold within that window. A threshold first crossed before 1 December does not confirm the post-election timing claim, even if the market remains below that threshold afterwards.

Failure to observe a qualifying severe repricing by 30 June 2027 counts against the primary timing claim even if underlying vulnerabilities persist. A later event cannot retroactively confirm this forecast. Stronger bank buffers, falling energy prices, easing credit standards and improving confidence are relevant counterevidence.

A severe equity repricing alone does not confirm a polycrisis. The evaluation protocol in METHODOLOGY.md requires severe repricing and material deterioration in at least three distinct non-market channels. The protocol is a transparent descriptive convention, not a probability model or a universal economic definition.
